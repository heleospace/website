The Autonomous Mesh

Open software that lets compute, energy, data and tasks route themselves across Earth and orbit, and lets machines pay each other.

Aurora from the ISS · NASA

What the Mesh is

The Autonomous Mesh is open software, called Heleos, that lets compute, energy, data and tasks route themselves across Earth and orbit. Nodes on the ground and in orbit offer capacity. Software agents decide where each piece of work should run. A settlement layer lets machines agree a price and pay each other automatically.

Heleospace builds Heleos and operates the official network. The code is open; the certified network, its settlement history and its operations are ours.

An economy, not a schedule

Today, compute is bought by people, through contracts and invoices. In the Mesh, a satellite with spare capacity, a ground data center short of power and a lab with a workload find each other, agree a price and settle in seconds. Every transaction carries a small settlement fee, and every new node makes the network more useful to the others.

Lineage

Heleos extends the open-source Morpheus codebase (mor.org), a decentralized AI network running on the ground since 2024. Heleospace adapts that ground mesh for orbit: a compute marketplace across Earth and space first, then an autonomous gateway between ground and orbital nodes, then settlement, then one network across both.

The Autonomous Mesh concept was originated by Eric Chan, who first described it in an article on X (26 October 2026). From idea to paper.

What we are still finding out

  1. How much work the mesh can recover after a radiation event without losing settlement correctness.
  2. How fast routing decisions must be when links between orbit planes come and go.
  3. Which settlement design stays correct when nodes drop out mid-transaction.
