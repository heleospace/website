Research · Field 03

Space is the hardest test lab there is

Beam tests and vacuum chambers predict how hardware behaves in space. Heleo-1 is designed to show how it actually does, over years, in a real orbit.

Station solar arrays across Earth's horizon · NASA

Overview

Commercial chips are fast and cheap, but nobody knows exactly how long they last in orbit until they fly. Ground tests narrow the question; flight data answers it. Heleo-1 is planned to carry commercial compute modules in a dawn-dusk orbit at about 550 km and to record how they behave, every day of the mission.

This is also the core of our own research. Our Year 1 workstreams on radiation, shielding and thermal-vacuum testing build the ground results that Heleo-1 will check in flight. See our research.

What you can do

Radiation and fault behaviour

Measure error rates, latch-ups and recovery for processors and memory, and compare flight data with beam-test predictions.

Thermal and power studies

Study efficiency and heat limits in vacuum, where heat can only leave as radiation.

Open orbital telemetry

Datasets of temperatures, power and error counts from Heleo-1, to be published for research.

Hosted experiment slots

Fly a small experiment on a Heleospace mission. By invitation, for partner labs.

Research questions we want to help answer

  1. How well do beam-test results predict the error rates commercial chips actually see in orbit?
  2. How does memory degrade over a multi-year mission, and how early can software detect it?
  3. How much compute can a radiator carry away at high density, and how should work be scheduled around it?
  4. What do software fault mitigations cost in speed and energy, and when are they worth it?
  5. Which shielding gives the best protection per kilogram for a given chip?

How a project runs

Every project starts on the ground and moves to orbit only when it is ready. You keep the same code and the same account at every stage.

StageStatusWhat happens
1. Ground meshAvailable nowAnalyse published results from our lab workstreams and run fault-injection studies on ground nodes.
2. Heleo-1 ground twinPlannedTest your software or fault mitigations on a testbed that mirrors the Heleo-1 compute pod.
3. Heleo-1 in orbitPlannedRun your code on Heleo-1, use its telemetry, or fly a hosted experiment by invitation.

Orbital access and hosted experiments are planned and subject to mission approval, licensing and export rules.

What we provide, what you bring

We provide

  • Ground testbeds and fault-injection tools
  • Access to the Heleo-1 ground twin
  • Orbital telemetry datasets from Heleo-1
  • Engineering support for hosted experiments

You bring

  • Hardware or software you want to test
  • A test plan with clear measurements
  • Beam-test or thermal-vacuum capability, if you have it
  • A plan to publish the results

Your team leads the work, owns its data and keeps the credit. Results that use Heleo Mesh or Heleo-1 are shared and cited, so the whole field moves faster.

Apply

Tell us about your lab, your question and what you want to run, on the ground or in orbit. Labs with beam-test, thermal-vacuum or chip-design capability are especially welcome.
