The problem

AI compute is outgrowing the ground.

Demand for AI compute keeps rising. The ground cannot add power, cooling and land fast enough, and nothing yet lets machines on Earth and in orbit work as one system.

01

Power

New AI data centers wait years for a grid connection.

02

Cooling

Every new site needs large amounts of water and cooling.

03

Land

Sites near spare grid capacity are scarce.

04

Coordination

Orbital and ground nodes have no shared way to route work and pay each other.

The physics

In orbit, the sun barely sets and space is the heat sink.

A dawn-dusk orbit keeps solar arrays in near-continuous sunlight, and heat leaves as radiation, with no water. The hard parts are radiation, heat at high density and launch cost, and that is where our research starts.

1,361W/m²Sunlight above the atmosphere, near-continuous in a dawn-dusk orbit
≈400W/m²Solar array output at roughly 30% cell efficiency
≈410W/m²Heat a 300 K radiator rejects per face in vacuum
<600kmLow Earth orbit; satellites deorbit by natural decay at end of life

How it works

One network. Machines trade compute, energy and data.

A satellite with spare capacity, a ground data center short of power and a lab with a workload find each other, agree a price and settle in seconds, with no person in the loop.

Layer 1

Autonomous Mesh

Helios, our open software, routes each piece of work to where it runs best, on Earth or in orbit.

Layer 2

Orbital and ground nodes

Solar-powered compute in low Earth orbit, linked with data centers on the ground.

Layer 3

Settlement

Machines pay and get paid for compute, energy, bandwidth and data, automatically.

Capital-light by design

Partners build the hardware. We build the network.

Specialist partners supply chips, satellite buses and launch. Heleospace owns the layers that compound: the coordination and the settlement.

  • HeleospaceAutonomous Mesh, settlement layer, node certification
  • PartnersLow-power chips, satellite buses, launch, ground sites
  • TogetherChip co-design tuned to mesh workloads and orbital heat limits

Why it lasts

Code can be forked. A working orbital economy cannot.

Hardware can be bought. A running network, with its nodes, settlement history and the labs that depend on it, takes years to build and grows harder to replace with every node that joins.

Network effect

Every node adds both supply and demand. Settlement history and node reputation stay in the network.

Testnet in Year 1

Radiation- and heat-aware routing

The mesh moves work away from radiation zones and hot nodes, and recovers it without losing a payment.

Ground testbed in Year 1

Open standard, certified network

Anyone can build on Helios. Only Heleospace certifies nodes for the official network.

Roadmap

Prove it on the ground. Then fly.

Years 1 and 2 are research-led, with university and government labs. The first orbital demonstration follows in Year 3. These are targets, not promises.

Year 1

Ground testbed

Lab validation, five research workstreams, settlement testnet.

Year 2

First pilots

Paying users on the ground mesh; machine-to-machine payments between partner nodes.

Year 3

Heleo-1

First orbital demonstration and first payments settled in orbit.

Year 4

Heleo-2 to Heleo-4

Inter-satellite links across orbit planes; commercial services.

Year 5

Open standard

The protocol for ground-to-orbit compute, with recurring settlement revenue.
