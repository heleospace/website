Machines

Machines that buy, sell and share compute

Robots, vehicles and drones generate more data than they can process on board. The Mesh gives them somewhere to send it, and a way to pay.

Photo: Akin Cakiner, Unsplash

Why machines need the Mesh

On-board compute runs out. Data does not.

A machine's own compute is limited by its battery and its heat. Fleets produce large volumes of driving and sensor data every day. Today a person signs a cloud contract on their behalf. In the Mesh, the machine buys what it needs, when it needs it, and settles automatically.

01

Limited on board

Battery and heat cap how much a robot or vehicle can compute while it works.

02

Data keeps growing

Cameras and sensors produce far more data than a machine can learn from on its own.

03

Pay as it goes

Machines find capacity on the ground or in orbit, agree a price and pay for verified work.

Robotaxi fleets

Learn from every trip, overnight.

A fleet ends the day with hours of driving data. The Mesh finds capacity to train on it while the vehicles charge.

  • Buys from the MeshOvernight training on the day's driving data; map updates
  • Sells backIdle on-board compute while parked and charging

Delivery and logistics fleets

Plan the whole fleet, not one route.

Fleet-wide route planning and simulation need more compute than any single vehicle carries.

  • Buys from the MeshFleet-wide route planning, simulation of new routes
  • Sells backSpare battery energy at depots, road and sensor data

Humanoid robots

Think harder when it matters.

A humanoid handles balance and movement on board, and sends heavy reasoning and learning to the Mesh.

  • Buys from the MeshHeavy reasoning, new skills downloaded when ready
  • Sells backTask data that improves shared models

Legged robots at remote sites

Inspect where there is no network.

Legged robots inspect pipelines, mines and power lines far from ground networks. A satellite link and orbital compute bring the analysis to them.

  • Buys from the MeshInspection analysis through satellite links
  • Sells backSite imagery and sensor readings

Drones and space robots

Process data where it is captured.

Drones and robots in orbit capture data far from any data center. Nearby nodes process it and send back only results.

  • Buys from the MeshProcessing close to where data is captured
  • Sells backSpare capacity while in sunlight

A night in the Mesh

A robotaxi parks. By morning it has learned.

An illustration of how a single job could flow through the Mesh. Times are an example, not a measurement.

02:00

Parks and charges

The vehicle uploads the day's driving data and posts a training job with a deadline and a maximum price.

02:01

Finds capacity

An orbital node in sunlight has spare compute. Its agent quotes a price; the vehicle's agent accepts and locks payment.

02:05

Trains

The job runs with checkpoints. If a node drops out, the Mesh moves the work to another node.

05:30

Verifies and pays

Checkpoints are verified and payment for verified work is released automatically.

06:00

Back on the road

The updated model is ready before the first ride of the day.

What stays on board

Real-time control never leaves the machine.

Even low Earth orbit adds tens of milliseconds and links come and go. Steering, balance and safety decisions always run on the machine. The Mesh is for work that can wait seconds, minutes or hours.

On the machine

Milliseconds matter

Steering, braking, balance, obstacle avoidance, safety stops and anything that must answer in under about 100 ms.

On the Mesh

Work that can wait

Training, map building, fleet learning, simulation, inspection analysis and heavy reasoning that can take seconds or longer.
